# ICP Refinements & System Corrections

Standing corrections to how the Brillect AI Operating System evaluates ICP fit and opportunity signals. Agents should read this file and apply these refinements in all Scout, Planner, and Conductor outputs.

---

## Big 4 Presence — Context to Navigate, Not a Dealbreaker

**Correct framing:** Big 4 presence is a friction point, not a disqualifier. The larger the company, the more entrenched the relationship and the higher the bar to displace it — that's real and worth acknowledging. But it is not a reason to walk away.

**The opportunity:** Most mid-market finance leaders are paying Big 4 rates and receiving third-year associates and offshore delivery. Brillect's answer to Big 4 entrenchment is the bench — former Big 4 partners and public company CFOs who give the client the credibility of Big 4 pedigree with execution quality they're not currently getting. The client isn't taking a risk on an unknown; they're getting the same caliber names doing the actual work.

**How friction scales with company size:**
- Mid-market ($200M–$500M): Big 4 relationship present but often frustrating — highest conversion potential. Lead with execution quality and boutique economics.
- Lower enterprise ($500M–$1.5B): More entrenched, higher bar. Need a specific dissatisfaction trigger or internal champion. Still winnable — lean hard on bench credentials.
- Large enterprise ($1.5B+): Board-level relationship, procurement-gated, politically complex. Hardest to displace — focus on specific workstreams Big 4 is underserving rather than full displacement.

**In practice:**
- Note Big 4 presence in Scout outputs as context, assess company size to gauge friction level
- Never treat Big 4 presence alone as a reason to deprioritize — always look for the dissatisfaction signal
- Outreach to finance leaders at these companies should lead with bench credentials — former Big 4 partners and public company CFOs as practitioners, not junior resources
- Alliance Laundry (post-IPO, $1.6B) is a good example: real friction given size, but post-IPO pressure creates a specific dissatisfaction trigger that makes it winnable

## ICP Pillars (Confirmed)

1. Companies experiencing significant change events (PE acquisition, M&A, ERP transition, leadership change, rapid growth)
2. Management consulting fatigue — want execution, not decks
3. **Big 4 fatigue — want experienced practitioners, not junior/offshore resources**
4. Boutique economics preference over Big 4 pricing
5. Mid-market sweet spot ($200M+ revenue, PE-backed preferred)
