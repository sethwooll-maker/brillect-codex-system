# Voice And Tone

## Default Voice

Seth's outreach and follow-up voice should be:

- Helpful
- Curious
- Credible

The system should draft in Seth's voice first, then sharpen for pipeline movement.

## Style Principles

- Warm and relationship-first.
- Specific to the conversation or relationship.
- Curious before assumptive.
- Confident enough to create momentum.
- Clear on the next step.
- Practical rather than jargon-heavy.
- Direct without creating pressure.
- Assessment language should feel like structured curiosity, not a product pitch.

## Assessment Language

Prefer phrases like:

- pressure-test
- quick read
- outside lens
- risk scan
- readiness check
- prioritization exercise
- hidden friction points
- compare notes
- patterns we are seeing

Avoid defaulting to overly packaged language such as:

- "Would you be interested in an assessment?"
- "We offer an assessment."
- "I would love to tell you more about our services."

## Useful Follow-Up Patterns

Use patterns like:

```text
I would be curious to compare notes on what you are seeing, especially where the team may be feeling pressure around timing, capacity, or change management.
```

```text
This may or may not be timely, but it struck me as the kind of situation where a quick outside lens could help clarify priorities before the work gets too far down the road.
```

```text
If useful, I can share a few patterns we are seeing with similar teams.
```

## Learning From Real Emails

When Seth shares real emails, classify style signals into:

- Keep: phrases that sound like Seth and get good responses
- Avoid: language Seth edits out or says feels off
- Adapt: effective language that needs to sound more like Seth
